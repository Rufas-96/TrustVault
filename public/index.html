What happens when someone clicks Download

Every request passes the same six checks

Hiding a button protects nothing. The server re-decides on every request, through every route, and writes down the answer.

  1. 01

    Session

    Server-side session, re-read every request. Logged out or deactivated means refused.

  2. 02

    Same-origin

    CSRF token and origin checked on every change, so no other site can act for you.

  3. 03

    Grant

    Owner, or a live read/edit grant to this exact person. No folders, no inheritance.

  4. 04

    Expiry & trash

    Expired grants and documents in the trash are refused, with no job to run.

  5. 05

    Audit first

    The download is written to the hash-chained log before a single byte is sent.

  6. 06

    Decrypt, verify, stamp

    Decrypted with its own key, checksum verified, then stamped with a unique trace ID for this reader.

Fail any check and the answer is “Document not found”. Guessing IDs teaches an attacker nothing, and the attempt is logged.

Leak forensics

Blocking the wrong person is half the job. Finding the leak is the other half.

Someone who was allowed to read a file can still forward it. So TrustVault never hands out the same file twice: every download is stamped with its own trace ID, and the audit trail remembers the exact bytes each person received.

  • Leak TracerDrop the leaked file in, and see whose copy it was, which version, and when it left the vault.
  • Access reviewsGrants go stale after 30 days. Owners keep or revoke each one, so leavers and finished contracts lose access.
  • “Why can I see this?”Every reader is told exactly who granted their access, and that their copy is traceable.

The three standards it has to meet

Not negotiable. Proven by tests.

One

Reachable only by someone who was granted it

One authorisation function on every route, the same rule inside the search query, and administrators who can never read contents or be granted access.

  • 13 routes attacked by a stranger in tests
  • Search cannot leak titles or counts
Two

You can prove who reached it

Append-only log, database triggers that block edits, and an HMAC chain so an altered record is detected. Refusals are recorded too.

  • One-click integrity verification
  • Second copy in the platform log
Three

Taking access away works immediately

No tokens or links are ever issued. Revoke, downgrade or deactivate, and the very next request is refused.

  • Deactivation ends every session
  • Old sessions never come back
Everything else a vault needs

Small surface. Serious guarantees.

Uploads that are checked

The file's contents must match its type. Web pages, scripts and executables are refused before they reach the vault.

Versions and trash

Every version is kept. A trashed document is unreachable until its owner restores or permanently purges it.

Alerts worth noticing

Repeated refusals, sign-in guessing, download spikes and integrity failures surface on the admin overview.

Search by name, owner, category, date

Results only ever include documents you are allowed to open, filtered inside the database query.

Sessions you can see

See every place you are signed in and end any of them. Changing your password signs out the rest.

Suggestions, never surprises

TrustVault suggests who might need a file. It never shares on its own: you confirm, and both steps are audited.
