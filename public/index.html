Built around three promises

Simple screens. Serious guarantees.

This is a security problem dressed as a document app. The screens are simple; what sits behind them is what matters.

Only the people you name

Access is granted per document to named people, as view or edit. No folders that quietly expose forty files. The server decides on every request, by every route.

Proof of who reached it

Uploads, views, downloads, edits, deletions and permission changes are recorded, and so are refused attempts. Edited records are detected.

Revocation that is immediate

No share links or long-lived tokens to chase. Remove a grant or deactivate a person and their very next request is refused.

Encrypted at rest

Every file is encrypted with its own key and bound to its record, so a copied or swapped file on disk is useless.

Versions and trash

Every version is kept. Deleted documents go to a trash nobody can open, and can be restored or permanently purged.

Suggestions, never surprises

TrustVault can suggest who might need a document. It never shares on its own: you confirm, and both steps are audited.

How it works

From upload to proof in three steps

Upload privately

An owner uploads a file. Its type is checked against its contents, it is encrypted, and nobody else can see it yet.

Grant by name

The owner gives named people view or edit access, optionally until a date, and can take it back at any moment.

Prove what happened

Every open, download, change and refusal lands in a hash-chained audit trail that administrators can verify.

Sign in with a demo account, revoke someone's access, and watch their next attempt get refused and recorded.
